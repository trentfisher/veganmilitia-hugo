"When people mix up entomology and etymology, it bugs me in ways I have no words for."

I have been using Ansible for a number of years.  There are a number of things I could critique, but the biggest one is how slow it is.  Running a bunch of ssh commands in a for loop would be *far* faster.  Of course, that's not as robust or flexible, but it is hard to understand why Ansible is *so* much slower.

I started joking that the word "ansible" is derived from a Latin word meaning "painfully slow".  Making etymology jokes is a sure sign of a nerd, but even more so is digging into the actual etymology.  I first thought that it was related to the word [runcible](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Runcible) from the [Edward Lear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Lear) poem [The Owl and the Pussy Cat](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Nonsense_Songs,_Stories,_Botany,_and_Alphabets/The_Owl_and_the_Pussy-cat).  While associating Ansible with nonsense was satisfying, it was not true.  It only took one more search to find that the word [ansible](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ansible) was invented by [Ursula Le Guin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ursula_K._Le_Guin) as the name of a faster-than-light communications device.

That's some big-league irony!  The slowest software on the planet named for a device that is faster than the laws of physics permit; which is, I guess, a nonsense of its own.

I would like to think that both Lear and Le Guin would be offended by being associated with this software.  I know I am.
